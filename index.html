What the river takes at 2,480 cfs

Every trip has a window: a low flow it needs and a high flow it cannot be guided above. That window is the whole business. At the sample reading of 2,480 cfs (11 Jun 2026), 5 of 6 trips would run and Pine Creek would be closed: it needs the river to drop before it can be guided.

How a trip runs

Four hours, start to finish

    1 · Basecamp, 30 minutes

    Meet at the shed, sign in, get a PFD and helmet that fits. Change rooms and lockers are on site. Leave the car keys in the box.

    2 · The talk, 15 minutes

    Your guide covers the four paddle commands, what to do if you go in, and the swim position. This is not a formality; it is the part that matters if the day goes sideways.

    3 · On the water

    Shuttle to the put-in, launch, and paddle as a crew. Rapids are called out loud and in advance, so you learn to read them as you run them.

    4 · Back at the shed

    Take-off, gear returned, dry bag returned to you. Most half-day trips are back at basecamp by early afternoon; full days by five.
