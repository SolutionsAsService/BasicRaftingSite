Safety

What we do so the day goes the way it should

Whitewater carries real risk and we do not pretend otherwise. This page is the whole of it: what we hold, what we give you, what we tell you before you launch, and what we need to know about you first.

On the record

Held current, every spring

  • Licensed Colorado river outfitter
  • Every guide current in Wilderness First Responder
  • Every guide current in Swiftwater Rescue Technician
  • US Coast Guard-approved PFD and helmet on every seat, every trip

Provided, every seat

Gear

  • US Coast Guard-approved personal flotation device, sized to you
  • Helmet, with a wetsuit and splash top when the water is cold
  • Paddle, throw bag, and a first-aid kit in every boat
  • A dry bag for anything that must stay dry
  • A spare paddle on every raft, and a safety kayak on the harder stretches

Wear

What to put on

  • Swimsuit or quick-dry shorts, and a synthetic or wool layer — no cotton against cold water
  • Shoes that strap on and will not come off in a swim. No flip-flops, no bare feet
  • Sunscreen and a hat that will stay on
  • Glasses or sunglasses with a retainer

Leave it

What not to bring

  • Anything you cannot bear to lose — the river keeps what it takes
  • Phones and cameras, unless they are in a waterproof case that is clipped on
  • Cotton hoodies and jeans
  • Alcohol, before or during a trip. We will not launch you

The talk, on the bank, before every launch

Four things you will be told

    1 · The commands

    Forward, back, hold, and get down. Four calls, practised on the bank before the boat moves, so you can hear them over water.

    2 · If you go in

    Feet up, feet first, face downstream, on your back. Do not stand in moving water. Swim to the boat or to the bank, whichever comes first.

    3 · The swim position

    What to do with your paddle, where to put your feet, and how the guide will reach you with a throw bag.

    4 · The plan if a boat flips

    Who goes for the swimmer, who stays with the raft, and where the group regroups. Rehearsed, not assumed.

Tell us before you book

Questions worth answering honestly

None of these rule you out. All of them change what we put you in.

Medical and physical

Heart or back conditions, pregnancy, recent surgery, anything that limits your swimming. Our guides are wilderness first responders, not paramedics, and the nearest hospital is a drive from every put-in we use.

Swimming

You do not have to be a strong swimmer for the float or Browns Canyon. You do for The Numbers, Pine Creek and the Gorge, where a swim in class IV water is a real possibility and helping with your own rescue is part of the deal.

Age and weight

Minimum ages run from 3 on the float to 16 on the Gorge, and are on every trip page. We do not set a maximum, but a PFD has to fit: tell us if you need a size outside the usual range.

Weather and flow

We launch in rain. We do not launch in lightning, and we move or cancel a trip when the gauge goes outside a trip's window. If we cancel, you rebook or you get your money back.
